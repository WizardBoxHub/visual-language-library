# Regional dress and embroidery

The Museum of Macedonia describes more than 70 forms of traditional dress surviving into the mid-20th century. Its costume collection includes 8,623 textile objects; most displayed examples date to the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Women produced much of this clothing, while specialist crafts and urban workshops supplied other components. The collection also documents Albanian, Turkish, Vlach and Roma dress.

This makes regional attribution essential. A Galichnik wedding garment should not stand in for every Macedonian community. Household textiles widen the study beyond ceremonial costume into weaving, bags, coverings and domestic use.

Source: [Museum of Macedonia, Ethnology](https://museum.org.mk/page/etnologija).

## How to study it

Compare neckline, cuff, hem, apron and belt separately. Record repeat direction, spacing, contrast and the relationship between dense ornament and plain cloth. Keep photographed display arrangements distinct from documented historical ways of wearing a garment.
