# Evidence and interpretation

Every reference should distinguish five things:

1. **Object or performance:** what is actually documented.
2. **Provenance:** findspot, maker or community, historical location, and present collection.
3. **Dating:** object date, photograph date and publication date are different.
4. **Interpretation:** who proposes a meaning, and on what evidence.
5. **Artistic response:** what you choose to adapt in your own work.

A diamond, rosette or paired bird can recur across places and periods. Resemblance is a starting point for comparison, not proof of a shared meaning or direct descent.

Use MCDNA as the vault name and Macedonia for its subject. Preserve institutional titles and catalogue terminology in citations so sources remain traceable. Ancient kingdoms, historical regions and the modern country are not interchangeable geographic categories.

For photographs, retain author, source page and licence. A public-domain historical object does not automatically make every photograph of it public domain. Related: [[Archive and museum reading room]].
